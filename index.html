Commercial Property Croydon in 2026: 231 Sales and the Busiest Outer Borough

Only Westminster sold more. In 2025 the London Borough of Croydon recorded 231 commercial and non-standard sales, 19.1 percent more than the year before and the biggest rise of any of the 33 boroughs. London as a whole went the other way, down 7 percent. Westminster’s 293 sales carried a median of 2,315,000 pounds. Croydon’s carried a median of 530,000 pounds. That gap is the point. The busiest market outside the centre is made of parades in Thornton Heath and South Norwood, workshops and surgeries in Purley and Coulsdon, and upper floors along London Road, bought one building at a time by people who mostly need a mortgage to do it. If you want to know what an ordinary commercial property purchase in the capital looks like, and how it gets funded, Croydon is the borough with the most evidence.

Why 530,000 pounds matters more than 385 million

Total value flatters the centre. Median price tells you what a normal buyer is buying.

At 530,000 pounds the typical Croydon lot is a single building. On the northern side of the borough that usually means a shop with one or two flats over it on one title. Further south it is a professional practice, a garage or a small trade unit that the occupier wants to own. The town centre has its own office and retail stock at larger sizes, and 62 of the borough’s sales were at 1 million pounds or more.

Our report makes the same point about London generally: growth in 2025 came from boroughs where the typical lot is between 500,000 pounds and 1 million pounds. Those are the sizes where a business buying its own premises, or a landlord buying one let building, is the natural purchaser. They are also the sizes with the widest choice of lender.

Croydon is not a cheap version of central London. It is a different market, where the typical building costs 530,000 pounds and more than a third of buyers bring a lender with them.

Worked example one: a business buying its premises at the median

Both examples are illustrative arithmetic on a hypothetical 530,000 pound building, the Croydon median. Neither is a quote.

Suppose a dental practice in Purley has rented its building for years and the landlord offers to sell at 530,000 pounds. Owner-occupier borrowing across our lender panel runs at 6.0 to 7.5 percent a year, up to 75 percent loan to value.

  • Loan at 75 percent: 397,500 pounds. Deposit: 132,500 pounds.
  • At 6.75 percent on a 20-year repayment term, the payment is about 3,022 pounds a month, or 36,269 pounds a year.
  • Lenders test that against the earnings of the business, looking for EBITDA cover of 1.3 to 1.5 times. At 1.3 times the practice needs EBITDA of about 47,150 pounds. At 1.5 times it needs about 54,400 pounds.
  • Stretch the term to 25 years and the payment falls to about 2,746 pounds a month, or 32,956 pounds a year, and the 1.3 times hurdle drops to about 42,840 pounds.

The longer term buys headroom on the cover test at the price of more interest over the life of the loan.

Worked example two: an investor buying the same building let

Now suppose the same 530,000 pound building is a shop with two flats above in South Norwood, bought as an investment. Investment and semi-commercial lending both price at 6.5 to 8.5 percent a year, and the test is rent, not earnings.

  • Loan at 70 percent: 371,000 pounds. Deposit: 159,000 pounds.
  • Interest at 7.5 percent: 27,825 pounds a year.
  • Interest cover of 140 percent needs rent of 38,955 pounds a year. Cover of 160 percent needs 44,520 pounds.

Set against the price, 38,955 pounds is a little under 7.4 percent of 530,000 pounds, and 44,520 pounds is 8.4 percent. Lenders also test at a stressed rate above the pay rate, so the real requirement is higher. A parade building that produces less than that will not reach 70 percent on rent alone, and the buyer puts in more cash.

Two details move the outcome. Where the flats supply a good share of the income, the case goes to the semi-commercial mortgages desks, which count both floors of rent. And where one title holds several shop units, lenders look hardest at the weakest tenant and the shortest lease, not the average.

Eighty-five financed purchases, and the bridge behind them

We matched each 2025 sale to the charges that companies file at Companies House when they give a lender security. In Croydon 85 of 231 sales had a matching charge, or 37 percent. Only Enfield was higher. The figure is a floor: individuals, partnerships, pension schemes and most overseas buyers file no charge, so the true share bought with debt is higher again.

The striking part is what kind of lender came first. Bridging lenders funded 35 percent of those 85 financed purchases, which is roughly 30 buildings. Across London the figure was 23 percent.

The 2026 register says the same thing more loudly. Between January and September 2026 companies registered 1,194 charges against Croydon property, up 4.6 percent, and 36 percent of them were to bridging lenders. No other borough has a higher bridging share. Those charges cover all property that companies borrow against, most of it residential investment, so they show how active lenders are in the borough, not commercial lending alone. They record security, not loan amounts.

Bridging is priced at 8.5 to 11.0 percent a year, or 0.70 to 0.95 percent a month. On the 371,000 pound loan above, 0.85 percent a month is 3,153.50 pounds, against 2,318.75 pounds a month of interest on a term loan at 7.5 percent. Every month on the bridge costs about 835 pounds more. In a borough where one financed purchase in three starts this way, the term exit should be agreed in outline before the bridge completes.

Planning: 178 changes of use and a 57 percent approval rate

Croydon received 338 commercial planning applications in the twelve months to June 2026, the fifth-highest count in London. Of those, 178 were changes of use. Of the applications decided, 57 percent were granted, against 71 percent across the capital.

Put plainly, more than four in ten decided commercial applications in Croydon were refused. That matters for funding because a term lender will normally lend against the existing lawful use and value, not the hoped-for one. A retail unit bought to become a nursery or a tuition centre is valued as a retail unit until the consent is in hand and the new use is trading. Once it is, the loan becomes a trading business mortgage at 7.0 to 9.0 percent a year and 60 to 70 percent loan to value, underwritten on accounts.

Our advice on any Croydon purchase that depends on planning is to make the numbers work on today’s use. If they do, the consent is upside. If they do not, the refusal rate is too high to borrow against a hope.

2026 so far, and what comes next

HM Land Registry had registered 72 Croydon sales completed in 2026 when the data was cut, level with Westminster and more than any other borough. That count is partial and cannot be set against a full year. The median of those sales is 540,000 pounds against 530,000 pounds for 2025, which suggests lot sizes have held. In the three months to the end of September 2026 the council received another 50 commercial planning applications.

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate from 3.75 percent to 4 percent at its meeting ending 4 November 2026, with the next decision due on 17 December 2026. The usual structure in this borough is a five-year fix inside a 15 to 25 year term, so a buyer agreeing terms now is fixing the cost through to the early 2030s. We test cover with room to spare for that reason.

FAQ

Is Croydon a good place to buy commercial property in 2026? The data cannot answer that for you, but it shows an active market: 231 sales in 2025, up 19.1 percent, at a median of 530,000 pounds, and lenders behind 37 percent of purchases. Whether a given building is a good buy depends on its tenant, its lease and its price.

How much deposit do I need for a commercial mortgage in Croydon? On indicative mid-2026 bands, 25 percent for an owner-occupier at the 75 percent ceiling and 25 to 35 percent for an investor. On the 530,000 pound median that is 132,500 pounds at 75 percent loan to value and 159,000 pounds at 70 percent.

Why are so many Croydon purchases funded with bridging loans? The records show the share, 35 percent of financed purchases, not the reason. In our experience bridges are used where a building is bought at auction, is part vacant or needs work before a term lender will take it. Each one needs a planned exit.

Will a lender fund a change of use in Croydon? A term lender will normally lend on the existing lawful use and value. With 57 percent of decided commercial applications granted, we structure the loan on the current use and treat the consent as a later refinance.
